# Broadcast overlay (`frontend/src/pages/overlay`)

- One OBS browser source, `/overlay/stream` (1920×1080, transparent page, over the tournament client), and a control dock, `/overlay/control`, both rendered by `App.tsx` outside the site's shell (no top bar, no session wait; `OverlayApp` is lazy-loaded). The overlay draws one of six pages, whichever the dock picks (`OverlayState.page`, `SCREEN_PAGES`), crossfading between them (`Screens`, `PageView`): gameplay and mappool after the designers' `design/tournament-client-gameplay.png` and `design/tournament-client-mappool.png`, and teams, winner, schedule and bracket drawn in the same language (slate ground with grain, Hecate Display for names and titles, extended light Helvetica Neue for figures, pills with tags over their top edge, dark chips, the crystal, the trophy glow at the foot). The gameplay page: holes in the frame (`OverlayStage`'s `holes`, a `clip-path` on the ground) let the client's two halves show through, each team's banner, name and star pips above its half, its bans and picks down its edge (`DraftColumn`), the map's live score under the client (`ScoreBar`) and the map on in the room in the bottom left (`MapStrip`: no HP, which the site doesn't store; title and artist scroll when they don't fit, as `Marquee` does everywhere). The mappool page: the stage's whole pool by category (`MapPill`: crimson tint and a struck crystal for a ban, the picker's colour washing in from the left and a filled crystal for a pick, the crystal's heart in the colour of the team that won the map once it's played; the map on in the room gets the frost ring) and the room's chat in the black well at the bottom left (`ChatWell`, the latest room's lines without `isRoomNoise`). Both name the stage and carry the trophy glow. The teams page (`TeamsPage.tsx`, before the match): each side's banner, name, qualifier seed ("Elemeler #3", `useQualifierSeeds`: the qualifier stage's statistics, `StatStanding.rank`, no Z-scores) and bracket record, its players with their ranks, and its earlier bracket matches (a filled crystal for a win, a hollow one for a loss), across a large "vs". The winner page (`WinnerPage.tsx`): the winner (a forfeit's other team, the saved score once concluded, or a score that already won (`standingOf`)) revealed in order (banner, name, players, score "karşısında" the loser, the counted maps in the order played as small pills whose crystal heart says who took each), under the title "Kazanan" with no stage or match details; where both teams go is stacked in the bottom left, leaving the bottom right free for the streamer's own overlay: the match each moves into from `winner_to`/`loser_to` with the team it meets there once decided (the stage until then), "Şampiyon" or "Elendi" without one; before there's a winner it shows the running score and "Maç bitince kazanan burada". The schedule page (`SchedulePage.tsx`, "Maçlar"): the current stage only (`currentStageId`: the match on stream's, else one with a room, else the earliest with matches to play), its unconcluded matches (live first, then by time) on the left and its results (latest first) on the right, six each, as capsules with both banners, the code and the time riding the top edge. The bracket page (`BracketPage.tsx`): the editor's board (positions, links, card size from `lib/bracket.ts` `BOARD`) in overlay cards, seen through the dock's camera (below). Colours are the `--color-ban|well|strip|chip*` roles and `--font-overlay-serif` (Noto Serif Display, condensed; `OverlayApp` loads it only on the overlay).
- Data: everything is the site's (`useMatchPage(matchId, { spectate: false })`, so the overlay doesn't count as a spectator; the chat included) and osu!'s (covers), except what only the streamer's machine knows, read from tosu's `/websocket/v2` (`tosu.ts`): each spectator client's live score, summed per team by matching its osu! id to the match's players (tosu's left/right guess only as a fallback), and the tourney manager's `ipcState`. The overlay and the dock only reach local services over WebSockets: OBS 33's Chromium blocks fetch from a hosted page to localhost but not WebSockets. tosu refuses a socket from an origin missing from its `ALLOWED_IPS` (tosu's dashboard → Settings → Allowed IPs, or `tosu.env` beside `tosu.exe`), so the site's bare hostname has to be added there.
- Beyond the match, the overlay reads the public bracket (`useOverlayBracket` in `useOverlayData.ts`, reloaded on every live change and polled while the socket is down) for the schedule, the bracket, the teams' histories and the winner's next match. It's held with the match (`useHeld`), so a result the site knows early doesn't reach the stream through the bracket either.
- The hold (`useBroadcastMatch.ts`): the room's bot hears a map finish on Bancho before the delayed spectator clients get there, so the site knows the result (stars, the map's winner, the next map and pick, the chat) while viewers still watch the map. While tosu says the clients are playing (`ipcState` 3), the overlay keeps the whole match as it was when the map started; it's released when they leave it (4 is every client on the results screen), or once the map's length plus two minutes have passed (a client closed mid-map). Without tosu nothing is held. The score bar keeps the last map's totals until the next map starts, since the results screen may zero the clients' scores. A star that lights on release plays the pip's entrance.
- Control (`obs.ts`, `control.ts`): the dock is meant as an OBS custom browser dock (Docks → Custom Browser Docks). It and the overlay connect to obs-websocket v5 (OBS 28+, `ws://127.0.0.1:4455` by default, with the password) and talk with `BroadcastCustomEvent`, every message tagged `app: "112servis-overlay"`: the overlay says `hello` when it connects, and the dock answers with `state` and sends it again on every change. The dock owns the state (the match on stream and the page; `completeState` fills in what an older one lacks) and keeps it in localStorage, and lists the bracket's live, upcoming and latest finished matches to pick from. So the backend never learns who streams. Scenes are left to OBS's own controls. The overlay's URL takes `?obs=`, `?pw=` and `?tosu=` (host:port), which the dock's "Kurulum" builds into the address it offers to copy, and `?match=<id>` / `?page=mappool` to show something without the dock (testing). Any other `/overlay/*` path goes to the dock.
- Page switching (`ControlDock.tsx`): "Sayfa" switches the page by hand (six buttons: Maçlar and Bracket for between matches, then the match's flow, Takımlar, Mappool, Oyun, Kazanan), and the dock reads tosu itself (its address under "Kurulum", default `127.0.0.1:24050`) to switch on its own, each behind a switch kept in localStorage: to gameplay as the clients start a map (`ipcState` becomes 3, which also drops a pending switch), to the mappool `RESULTS_FOR_MS` (20 s) after they go from playing to every one of them on the results screen (3 → 4), and to the winner once the match on stream is concluded (seen in the dock's bracket, which is also polled while the socket is down): if the clients are still playing its last map then (the referee usually concludes on Bancho's result, ahead of the delayed clients), the results' switch goes to the winner instead of the mappool; otherwise a pending switch is retargeted, or it switches at once. The countdown shows in the dock ("Mappool'a 14 sn", "Kazanan sayfasına 14 sn") with "Şimdi geç" and "Sonuçlarda kal"; choosing a page by hand or turning the switch off cancels it. The header says whether OBS and tosu are connected.
- The bracket camera (`bracketBoard.ts`, shared by the dock and the overlay so both agree on the board's bounds and what fits): `OverlayState.camera` is the board point at the view's centre and a zoom, or null for the whole board (`fitCamera`); the dock moves it ("Bracket kamerası", shown only while the bracket page is selected: a d-pad with "Bracket'in tamamı" in its middle, zoom in and out, "Maça odaklan" for the match on stream; arrow buttons and zoom act once on press and then repeat in smaller steps while held, `HoldButton`; with the pad focused the keyboard's arrows, +, - and 0 do the same), clamped so the view's centre stays over the board. The overlay glides to each new camera with a critically damped spring per axis (zoom on a log scale) written straight to the board's transform every frame (`useSmoothCamera`), so held steps blend into one motion without re-rendering; the first view once the bracket arrives is snapped, not glided to.
