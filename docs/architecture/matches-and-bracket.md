# Matches, qualifiers and the bracket

- `matches` holds what every match has: `starts_at` (only qualifiers require it), one `referee_id`, one `streamer_id` (never a lobby's), staff-only `referee_notes` and `concluded_at`. Its osu! multiplayer rooms are in `match_rooms` (`mp_id` is the primary key, so a room belongs to one match; rendered as `osu.ppy.sh/community/matches/<id>`), oldest first by `added_at`. Usually there's one, but a room that breaks mid-match is replaced and the old one stays listed. Responses carry them as `rooms: [{ mp_id, url }]`, and the frontend shows them with `RoomLinks` (public) and `RoomsEditor` (`pages/admin/MatchControls.tsx`, for adding and removing). `kind` is a `match_kind` enum (`qualifier`, `bracket`); each kind adds its own tables. The shared running routes (referee, rooms, notes, conclude, reopen) are in `handlers/matches.rs` / `db/matches.rs`; conclude and reopen hand bracket matches to `services/bracket.rs`.
- Qualifiers are lobbies: hosts create them by time, and teams sign up through `qualifier_entries` (`team_id` is the primary key, so one lobby per team), at most `LOBBY_SIZE` (7) per lobby. Each lobby has a fixed letter (`matches.lobby_index`, 1 = A, 27 = AA, spelled by `lobby_name` in `views.rs`), the one after the last lobby's when it's created, so adding, moving or deleting a lobby never renames another (a deleted one leaves a gap). The site and Discord call it "Lobi B"; its osu! room is `112S: (Qualifiers) vs (Lobby B)`, the tourney client's `(Team) vs (Team)` form like a bracket match's `112S: (Team A) vs (Team B)`.
- Either player can join, switch or leave until their current and target lobby start (`starts_at`) or are concluded. Signups lock the target lobby row so a lobby can't overfill. Hosts can move or remove any team at any time, still within the size limit.
- Running a match (`/api/matches/:id/*`): a referee takes a free match or gives up their own, which makes them its referee: the one Discord pings, asks to confirm a new time and lists on the match. Running it isn't limited to them: any referee (or host) can, so one can step in when the assigned one can't (`require_runs`, `canRun` on the referee page, the bracket panel and the lobby cards). A referee or a host adds or removes rooms (`POST /api/matches/:id/rooms`, `DELETE …/rooms/:mp_id`; removing one keeps its saved chat), writes notes and concludes it (which needs a room). After conclusion only notes stay editable for the referee; hosts can still change the rooms (a concluded match keeps at least one), reassign the referee and reopen it.
- `referee_notes` must never reach `/api/public/qualifiers`; it is only on the `StaffLobby` struct. The referee's Discord is public on purpose (players contact them there), as is every staff member's; players' Discord accounts are staff-only. The `teams_frozen` flag does not affect qualifier signups.
- Bracket matches (`handlers/bracket.rs`, `db/bracket.rs`): `bracket_matches` adds a side (`winners`/`losers`) and a stable number within it (shown as W1, L3; new matches get max+1), a position on the editor's board (`pos_x`, `pos_y` in 20px board units, so cards snap to a grid; unbounded, so either can be negative; every card is 12×5 units), two team slots, the score and a `stage_id` (the stage, and so the map pool, it's played in; `ON DELETE SET NULL`, editable even once concluded). Who streams it is `matches.streamer_id`, beside the referee, for bracket and potential matches alike (a lobby has none), so taking, giving up and assigning a stream is the same code for both (`db::matches::claim_stream`/`release_stream`/`set_streamer`). `bracket_links` says where a match's winner and loser go (target match + slot 1/2); the PK and `bracket_links_target_key` give each outcome one destination and each slot one source, and edits reject links that loop back.
- Concluding a bracket match needs both teams, a score with a winner and a room, and writes the winner and loser into their linked slots in one transaction. Reopening (host-only) takes them back out, unless a host has changed that slot since. Both refuse while a linked target is concluded. A concluded match's teams, links and score can't be edited until it's reopened.
- Forfeits: a referee or a host can conclude a bracket match by forfeit (`POST /api/bracket/:id/forfeit` with the slot that forfeited, from the referee page's "Hükmen bitir…" or "Hükmen…" in the bracket editor's panel) when a team doesn't show up or the match can't be finished. It needs both teams but no room or score; `bracket_matches.forfeit_slot` records the team that forfeited, the other one wins (`outcome` in `services/bracket.rs`, `winningSlot` in `lib/bracket.ts`), and the score shows as W / FF everywhere (`scoreHalves`, `messages::result_line` for Discord). Reopening clears it.
- Potential matches (`backend/src/potentials.rs`, `db/potential.rs`): some weeks a team plays twice, its second opponent decided by another match of the same week, so the teams have to agree on a time before they know who they'll meet. When an unconcluded bracket match's empty slot waits (through `bracket_links`) on an unconcluded match of the **same stage** whose two teams are known, every pairing of the teams that can reach it is a potential match: a `matches` row of kind `potential` (time, referee, streamer) plus `potential_matches` (its bracket match and the two teams in slot order). A slot waiting on another stage or on a match missing a team makes none (that would be a third match in the week); a reset fed twice by one match is one pairing. `potentials::pairings` (pure, unit-tested) says which should exist, and `db::potential::sync` reconciles them inside every transaction that changes the bracket's teams (update, delete, conclude, reopen, forfeit, team disband, stage delete via `resync_potentials`, dev seeds, and once at startup), after `db::potential::lock` (an advisory lock taken before any row lock). New ones start on their bracket match's time, referee and streamer (`follow_time`/`follow_referee`/`follow_streamer`: changing the bracket match's moves the ones still on the old value); a pairing that can no longer happen is set aside (`potential_matches.removed_at`), not deleted: it keeps its time, staff, proposals, notifications and predictions, its Discord channel is archived (`MatchInfo.archived`), and every reader leaves it out (the lists, `find`/`lock_schedulable`, `find_match`, the notifications, the pick'em and its pickers, Discord's open and upcoming matches; the `follow_*` updates still move it, but only report the live ones). Once the bracket match has both teams, the matching one is promoted: its time, referee, streamer, open proposals, notifications and pick'em predictions move onto the match (predictions left on the match for other teams are dropped), and it's set aside too. When the match that decided it is reopened, a set-aside pairing that can happen again comes back as it was, same row and channel (`restore`); the one the match had taken over (`Before`) also gets back the predictions and proposals and takes the match's current time, referee and streamer (`adopt`). Only deleting the bracket match or a team deletes the rows. Potential matches have no room, score, notes, referee page or public page (`load_match` and `find_room` refuse them), but referees and streamers take them like matches (`PUT /api/matches/:id/referee`, `PUT /api/bracket/:id/streamer`), their teams reschedule them (`lock_schedulable`; `/team` marks them "Olası"), they're in the notifications (`potential`, `bracket_match_id` to open), and each gets a Discord channel at once (no time needed; "L20 (olası): A vs B", card explaining it, no reminder or announcement). `BracketMatch.potentials` carries them; the grid shows the one match with "Daha kesinleşmedi" for its time, while the list (branch rows under the match), the calendar (dashed chips), the fixture rows (landing, team matches) and the pick'em ballot show each pairing (`potentialRows`/`withPotentials` in `lib/bracket.ts` make stand-in matches with `potential_of`, linking to their bracket match), and the admin match panel lists them with staff controls. In the pick'em they're predicted while their stage is open; only the pairing that happens ever scores, since its predictions move to the match.
- Live scores: a match is live (`isLive` in `lib/bracket.ts`) once its time has come and it has a room, until it's concluded; its `team1_score`/`team2_score` are then the running score. The room's bot saves it as it changes (Bancho bots, below), the bracket views mark live matches with `components/LiveMark.tsx` (the glowing "Canlı" dot) and the fixture rows show the running score instead of "vs". Saving a score also queues a `SyncMatch`, and the Discord match card shows the running score ("Canlı skor") while the match has a room.
- Live updates (the hub in `live.rs`, its socket in `handlers/live.rs`, `live/LiveContext.tsx`): every tab keeps one WebSocket to `GET /api/live` (anyone, Origin checked), mounted at the app root. The score route pushes `score` with the new figures after it commits; the other bracket and match changes (create, edit, delete, staff, rooms, conclude, reopen, forfeit, an accepted reschedule) push `bracket`, and pages reload the bracket (gathered over 250 ms, and again after a reconnect). The landing page, `/bracket`, the admin bracket (unless moves are still waiting to be saved) and team match histories listen through `useLiveBracket`. The referee page listens on its own (`useRefereeRoom.ts`): `score`, `match` and `referee` for its match and every `bracket` reload it, so every referee and host on a match sees the same. `lib/useLiveRefresh.ts` polls every 30 s only while the socket is down (the referee page polls itself then). When adding a bracket change, call `state.live.bracket_changed()` (or `score`) after the commit; a change to one match the bracket doesn't show (bans, picks, hand-awarded results, a lobby's teams) calls `state.live.match_changed(id)` instead, which only that match's public page and referee pages react to (`useLiveBracket` ignores it); a change only the referee page shows (its room options, "Sayma", an osu! sync) calls `state.live.referee_changed(id)`, a `referee` event sent to staff only. A tab can also `watch` a match (`watch` in `LiveContext`, sent again when its rooms change, since the server looks them up then): it's then sent `chat`, every line the room's bot saves in that match's rooms (filtered per tab by room), and `room`, the room as the bots read it (`LiveRoom`, below) whenever it changes. The bancho manager also hears every change the pages are told about (`Live::changes`: a match's id, or `All` for `bracket`) and reads that match again; staff hear `bancho` when a bot connects, drops or takes a room (the bots page reloads). A signed-in tab hears `notifications` when that user's notifications change: every write in `db/notifications.rs` returns the users whose rows it added or dropped (`RETURNING user_id`), and the caller passes them to `state.live.notifications_changed` after the commit (marking read and answering send the user's own id, for their other tabs). Rows changed indirectly (a cascade, a potential match's moving to its bracket match) send nothing; the next count read catches them. A tab's `watch`es are applied at most every 500 ms (each reads the match's rooms; the latest waiting one wins), and its messages are capped at 4 KB. Watching also counts the tab among the match's spectators unless it says `spectate: false` (the referee page, below) (only a match that has a room; nothing stored): every tab watching it hears `spectators`, the signed-in viewers by osu! name (each user once) and how many tabs nobody is signed in on (`guests`), whenever that changes; the public match page shows it in its header's live capsule, beside the page's one "Canlı" (`pages/match/MatchSpectators.tsx`: the count, and the names on a click).
- Referee-page presence: the referee page tells the socket which match it shows (`viewing`, referees and hosts only), and staff who can open the admin bracket hear each match's list of viewers (`presence`, and `presence_all` on connect), each user once. Nothing is stored; a closed tab or socket drops out, and the socket closes on `pagehide` so a page in the back/forward cache doesn't linger. The admin bracket shows it as an eye with the count on grid cards, list rows and the match panel (`components/bracket/RefereeWatchers.tsx`, names on hover, focus or tap), and the referee page lists everyone on it under its header.
- Hosts build the bracket; a referee (or a host) enters the score. Streamers take a free match or give up their own like referees do, but not a match their own team plays in; hosts can assign anyone holding Streamer. `/api/bracket` (with notes and player Discords) is for referees and hosts; the other staff (streamers can play) get the public data in the admin tab.
- Players reschedule their own bracket matches (`handlers/reschedule.rs`, `db/reschedule.rs`, shown by `components/MatchReschedules.tsx` on `/team`): `match_reschedules` holds times either team of a match has proposed (any number, up to `PROPOSAL_LIMIT` (10) per team per match, one row per time). Any player of the other team accepting one sets `matches.starts_at` and drops every proposal for the match, from both sides; either team can cancel or decline. Only for matches with both teams, until the match has a room or its current time has passed (`open`); the match row is locked for proposing and accepting. A host taking a team out of a match drops that team's proposals, and concluding drops the rest. `teams_frozen` doesn't affect it. An accepted move notifies the staff and the players (below).
- Notifications (`handlers/notifications.rs`, `db/notifications.rs`, the bell in `components/NotificationBell.tsx`, shown in the top bar for every signed-in user) are one `notifications` row per recipient, about a match (`match_id`) or a team (`team_id`), with what happened copied in (times, ranks, team value) and the match's code and team names read when listed. When players move a match, its referee and streamer each get a `confirm_time` and every other host a `match_rescheduled`; a newer move deletes the unanswered `confirm_time`s of the older one. Confirming only records the answer. Declining takes them off the match (`referee_id` / `streamer_id` set to null) and sends `staff_declined` to everyone who could take the slot: the holders of Referee for a referee, the holders of Streamer who don't play in the match for a streamer (a host only if they hold that role). An answer needs the user to still hold that slot and the match to be unconcluded and still at that time (`actionable` in the list); it locks the match before the notification, like accepting a reschedule. The bell shows `/api/notifications/count`: unread notifications plus unanswered actionable ones; opening the panel marks everything read. It reads it again when the live socket says the user's notifications changed (`notifications`, below) or the bracket did (`bracket`: a match moved or concluded can make a question no longer actionable, and it's what a reconnect sends); an open panel reloads its list instead, marking what arrived read. It polls every minute only while the socket is down. A host moving an unconcluded match to a future time in the bracket editor asks its referee and streamer the same way (`ask_confirm_time`, minus the host who moved it; `by_host` on the notification, and a `ConfirmTime` job for the Discord DMs), but tells no other hosts; other host edits notify no one.
- Players get three kinds, which link to `/team` (hosts' copies of the rank one to `/admin/teams`). `reschedule_accepted`: an accepted proposal tells every player of both teams but the one who accepted, with the old and new time (`notify_players_rescheduled`); a newer move replaces their unread one. `reschedule_proposed`: a proposal notifies both players of the other team, unless they still have an unread one for that match, so one notice covers every proposal made before it's read; the list shows how many of the opponent's proposals are still open (`pending_proposals`). `rank_over_limit`: whenever ranks are written (`rank_refresh::refresh`, which every refresh path goes through, and the osu! login upsert), `rank_refresh::notify_over_limit` checks the teams of the users whose rank changed and tells the players and the hosts when a team that was within the limit (or unranked) is now over it, naming the member whose value rose the most (`over_limit_cause`, unit-tested). A team that stays over isn't told again; one that drops back under and goes over again is. Losing a rank (`unranked`) doesn't notify.
