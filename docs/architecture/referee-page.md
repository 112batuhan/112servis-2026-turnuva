# Referee page and Bancho IRC

- `/admin/referee/:matchId` runs a match (there's no list page: referees open it from a lobby on `/admin/qualifiers`, or from a bracket match on `/admin/bracket`, where every view has a `RefereeRoomLink` and so does the match panel): the teams with their Discords, the osu! room's slots, the Bancho chat and the map pool. `GET /api/matches/:id/room` (`handlers/referee.rs`, referees and hosts) returns all of it in one response: a lobby's teams in signup order, or a bracket match's two teams with `team1_id` blue and `team2_id` red (the room seats blue in slots 1-2 and red in 3-4; `SIDE_COLOR`/`SIDE_SLOTS` in `lib/bancho.ts` are the one place that says so, and every head-to-head on the site, Discord's 🔵/🔴 included, follows it; the public site shows blue as green and red as purple, `--color-team-*` in `styles/tokens.css`, and the referee page's root `.osu-team-colors` puts osu!'s own blue and red back), plus the stage's pool (a lobby uses the `is_qualifier` stage). Maps come from the `Public*` structs, so they carry no editor notes.
- The site runs the rooms with its own bots (`backend/src/bancho`, next section); a referee drives the room from the page, every command going through `POST /api/matches/:id/room/command` (`RoomCommand` in `bancho/mod.rs`) to the room's bot, and talks in it from their own account. So the page works without a Bancho login, and a referee who drops or leaves takes nothing with them: whoever opens the page next runs the same room. Hosts' osu! accounts are used as the bots, and a bot can't join another bot's room, so hosts don't join rooms at all: their chat input sends to the current room through its bot (`say`, hosts only: signed `[Name] ` unless the room's bot is their own account; an `!mp` command goes as it is, except `make`, `addref` and `removeref`, which go through the room settings), and their Bancho login is folded behind "Bancho'ya bağlan", for private messages. Commands need the latest room's bot in the room (`LiveRoom.rooms[].access == "in"`); otherwise `botBlocker` in `useRefereeRoom.ts` says why and the match column shows a notice with the way out (`BotNotice` in `LobbyPanel.tsx`: a room made elsewhere waits for its maker's `!mp addref <bot>` and "Tekrar dene" rejoins; a bot that dropped, or a room without a bot, moves to "Başka botla yeni oda aç"). The room bar carries the bot's name and where it stands (`BotBadge`).
- Browsers can't speak IRC, so `GET /api/irc` (`handlers/irc.rs`, the sessions behind it in `bancho/relay.rs`, referees and hosts, Origin checked against `FRONTEND_URL`) is a WebSocket relay to `BANCHO_IRC_ADDR` (default `irc.ppy.sh:6667`) for the referee's own account. It logs in with the referee's own osu! IRC credentials, which stay in memory and are never stored or logged. It answers PINGs, paces outgoing lines (at most 8 per 5 s) and forwards private messages plus our own JOIN/PART as JSON; room lines aren't forwarded (every page has the bot's log) and it saves nothing. Bancho rejects a bad login by sending a MOTD and hanging up, so any close before `001` counts as a failed login. Each user has at most one Bancho connection (a session in `bancho::relay::IrcUsers`, in `AppState`), shared by all of their tabs. A tab's first message is `login` (joins the session if there is one, whatever it typed, otherwise starts it) or `resume` (joins it, or gets `no_session`); a tab joining later gets `connected`, its joined channels and its private messages as a backlog, then every event. The session ends when its last tab closes or any tab sends `quit` ("Bağlantıyı kes"). Logging in with a bot's account is refused (`bot_account`): Bancho keeps one IRC connection per account, so it would take the bot off. All sessions come from the server's address, so after 3 failed logins a user waits 5 minutes before trying again (an attempt counts as failed until `001`, unless it never reached Bancho). A tab with a match open sends `attend` (again when the match's rooms change, and after reconnecting; `attend` in `IrcContext`): the room's bot then `!mp addref`s the referee, and once BanchoBot confirms, the manager has the session JOIN the room's channel (`SessionLink`/`SessionRequest` in `bancho/relay.rs`), so what they type there goes out from their own account. When the last of their tabs on it closes (or the session ends) they leave the channel, and if the bot added them for this they're `!mp removeref`'d two minutes later (`REF_GRACE`), unless they came back. Bot accounts are never added this way.
- A relay socket closes with a `CloseReason` (`relay.rs`, snake_case), which the page shows in Turkish (`CLOSE_REASON_TEXT` in `irc/IrcContext.tsx`).
- `irc/IrcContext.tsx` holds the tab's relay socket: the connection's state, the channels it's in and the private conversations; it's mounted in `AdminLayout`, so the connection survives switching pages. For referees it resumes the user's session on load and again when another tab of the browser comes online (a `BroadcastChannel`), so only the first tab asks for the password. The session keeps its last 500 private messages in memory (never in the database) and sends them to a tab that joins as `past_message`s, numbered by `seq` so a tab rejoining the same session (`connected.session`) skips the ones it has; they get negative ids, like loaded room lines, so they don't count as unread. Closing a DM tab closes it in every tab (`close`, answered with `conversation_closed`).
- `pages/admin/referee/useRefereeRoom.ts` owns the page's data: the room endpoint, whose `live` is the bots' reading of the room (kept current by `room` events), each room's saved chat plus every `chat` line streamed after it (the page always `watch`es its match with `spectate: false`; `lib/roomChat.ts`: loaded lines get negative ids and streamed ones positive, so only new ones are unread), and the actions, which call the command route and the match routes. "Oda oluştur" (`POST /api/matches/:id/room/create`) has the least busy bot make the room and set it up (below); "Yeni oda aç" does the same to replace a broken room (the old one stays listed with its chat); the match's latest room is the one being played. In a bracket match the referee also marks bans and picks on the pool: `match_bans` and `match_picks` hold one row per pool map with the team that banned or picked it (one of the match's two), set and removed through `PUT`/`DELETE /api/bracket/:id/bans|picks/:map_id` by a referee or a host until it's concluded, and returned as `bans` and `picks` by the room endpoint. A map can't be both banned and picked (the inserts check). The pool panel (`lib/matchPool.ts` has the shared labels and team colours) offers each map the buttons of the next step of the stage's order (`stages.draft_order`, above), ban or pick, for both teams, the one whose turn it is first (the other dimmed, for a roll the order didn't foresee); pick buttons once the order has run out, none for TB; a pick also has the bot set the map. Clicking a pool map sends `!mp map` and `!mp mods` (NF added unless turned off; categories named FM/TB get Freemod).
- The page is a cockpit one screen tall under a one-line header: pool | match | chat, each scrolling on its own. On a phone (the page under 760px wide) it shows one of them at a time under a tab bar (Havuz / Maç / Sohbet) that sticks below the top bar (`--nav-height`, set by `NavBar`), each tab showing where its panel stands: the ban count or the next picker, the running score with the live dot while a map plays, and the chat's unread lines or the Bancho connection; the chat keeps lines unread while it's behind another tab (`visible` on `ChatPanel`). The pool column (and the public match page's) shows the draft as the order lays it out (`DraftStrip` in `components/match/PoolView.tsx`, from `matchDraft` in `lib/matchPool.ts`): the bans and picks made as team-coloured chips, the next step ringed, the ones to come dashed, then "sırayla"; the chat column starts with the referee's Bancho login (`IrcLogin`, for talking only) and has a tab per room of the match (its bot's log, read-only until the referee's connection is in its channel) and one per private conversation. The match column (`LobbyPanel.tsx`) reads top to bottom: the room bar (its links, the bot, and behind the settings button the rooms, the options, the room's referees (`RoomReferees`: who Bancho has as one, each removable, and a select to add any holder of Referee, Streamer or Commentator, each named with their role, except a player of the match) and "Odayı kapat"), the map clock, the players, the commands, then the score over the map results. It lists the match's players by team (`RoomPlayers.tsx`, blue beside red; the teams and the room's slots in one view), each pinned to their row in slot order (`RosterPlayer.home`: team 1's players take 1 and 2, team 2's 3 and 4, in the order the team lists them) whatever slot they're in, with the slot they're in as a chip in the colour of the team the room has them on, their ready state or score, the mods they picked under freemod (as acronyms, from the last `!mp settings`; "?" until read), their Discord and, while they're away, an invite button; anyone in the room who isn't in the match is listed after. A player out of their slot or colour gets a danger dot on their slot chip (where they belong is in its tooltip), and clicking any slot chip opens the slot picker: every slot with who holds it, the two colours and "Yerine gönder" (the `move` command; the bot swaps through a free slot, see `move_commands`). "Yerleri düzelt" places every player (`place_all`). Only the match's players and holders of Host, Referee or Streamer (`useRoomStaff`/`staffRole` in `lib/roomStaff.ts`, from `GET /api/public/staff`, for display; the bots read the staff themselves) belong in the room. Everyone else in it is listed under the players (staff named by their role, with no button; anyone else with "Odadan at" and, for several, "Hepsini at"), and the public page names the staff there too. The results list each finished map of the pool (the bots' `LiveFinished`: matched to a pool map by beatmap id, and when the pool has that beatmap more than once, by the mods it was played with, then by the picks; anything else, like a warmup, is left out and not counted) with its pool label, each player's score and mods, the winner in its team's colour and who picked it; "Sayma" leaves a pool map out of the score (`match_ignored_maps`, `PUT /api/matches/:id/ignored-maps`, keyed by source, room and the map's place in it). The referee can also award a map (or a point without one) to a team by hand, with an optional note, for a map the room didn't settle: `match_manual_results`, added and removed through `POST /api/bracket/:id/results` and `DELETE …/results/:result_id` by a referee or a host until it's concluded, returned as `manual_results` by the room endpoint, counted with the room's results and listed in the Discord results post. The score is the bots' count (`LiveRoom.tally`), shown at the top of the score sheet (`MatchHistory.tsx`) and saved by the room's bot (below); "Sitede güncel" while the site has it, otherwise the site's score with "Skoru kaydet" (`POST /api/matches/:id/room/save-score`). With a best of, the sheet's head carries "Bo9" by the title and each team's wins as pips under its figure (`firstTo` of them), and under the head a race line: match point, a team that has won (the referee page's "Maçı bitir" moves there; concluding before a team has won warns), or a score over the best of. At the tiebreaker nobody is offered as the next picker and the pool's phase says "Tiebreaker". Once a bracket match is concluded the page lists its saved results (`map_results` with `players`, from `match_map_results` and `match_map_scores`; a row's pool map goes null if the map is deleted, keeping its scores) instead; the Discord results post lists them. When the chat missed or misread something, the referee clicks "Maç linkinden eşitle" (`POST /api/matches/:id/osu-sync`): the server reads osu!'s record of the rooms (`osu_api/matches.rs`; a room osu! has no record of keeps its chat), which takes the place of the chat's maps up to its last one, for everyone, and saves the score counted from it (`match_room_settings.osu_synced_at`; read again after a restart). "Maçı bitir" under the score sheet concludes the match (a bracket match with the bots' score, saved first if the site doesn't have it, and only with a winner). The commands also say whose ban or pick is next (the pages read it with `draftOf` in `lib/draftOrder.ts`, the bots with `turn_of` in `bancho/tally.rs`, kept as `LiveRoom.next_turn` and not sent: the stage's order against the bans and picks in the order they were marked; the referee can override the team until the next ban or pick: `match_room_settings.next_pick_team_id`, set through `PUT /api/matches/:id/next-pick` and cleared by marking or taking back a ban or pick) and "Skoru odaya yaz" has the bot send the score and that line (`announce_score`; `scoreMessage` in `lib/matchPool.ts` is its preview). The score sheet makes the two teams columns that run from the score down through every map, newest first: each map's totals sit in them with the winner's cell lit (an ignored map's stay unlit), and an opened map lists its players with each score in their team's column; the newest is open, the others open on click. While a map is played, the match column counts down its pool length (already mod-adjusted) from when the bot saw the latest room's "The match has started!" (`map_started_at`), so a reload keeps the clock; a map outside the pool counts up. The room commands end with "Yayıncıyı hakem ekle" for a bracket match with a streamer (`RefereeRoom.streamer`): `!mp addref` so their tournament client shows the chat on stream, pressed ("Yayıncı hakem") once Bancho says it's done, and `!mp removeref` from there. Staff in the room who are its referees say so under their name. What the room keeps besides bans, picks and results (the room options, "Sayma", the last osu! sync, the next-pick override) is saved per match (`match_room_settings`, `match_ignored_maps`; `handlers/referee.rs`, `db/referee.rs`), returned as `settings` and `ignored_maps` by the room endpoint, changed by a referee or a host until it's concluded (the controls are disabled for everyone else), applied on the page at once and saved in the background; the bots read it from there. The page is keyed by match. Players address commands as `#<osu_id>`, and the slot parser accepts negative ids so the dev accounts work.
